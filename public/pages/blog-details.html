5 Common Habits That Destroy Your Kitchen Drain Traps

Your kitchen sink drain works hard every single day, but behind the scenes, seemingly harmless cooking habits can lead to stubborn grease plugs, foul sewer smells, and expensive emergency pipe repairs.

1. Pouring Warm Cooking Oils & Fats Down the Drain

Liquid bacon grease or frying oil might flow easily when hot, but as soon as it hits the cold P-trap beneath your sink, it solidifies into a thick, waxy coating that traps food particles and closes off pipe diameter.

Master Plumber Maintenance Tip:

Pour used cooking oils into an empty metal tin or glass jar and let them cool before disposing in the trash. Never rinse grease down sinks even with hot water and dish soap.

2. Grinding Fibrous Vegetables in Garbage Disposals

Celery stalks, corn husks, onion skins, and potato peels contain long, fibrous threads that wrap around disposal blades and jam the motor impeller. Always throw fibrous food scraps into compost bins.

3. Dumping Coffee Grounds Down the Basin

Coffee grounds do not break down in water. Instead, they accumulate in drain elbows and form a heavy sediment paste resembling wet cement.
